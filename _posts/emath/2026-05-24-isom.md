## My Mushroom Trip

During my mushroom trip in the Netherlands, I experienced something that is hard to describe. The trip was weird and overwhelming; I took the planned amount of truffles and laid down in my hotel with my eyes closed. It is hard to explain what I saw or went through, I was teleporting from and into different dimensions of being. Each dimension had different characteristic or things that make up what it is _like to be_ in that dimension. From a 2D game-like pixelated world to a fluid-like being that has no grasp of any containment. but through all of these, one pervading thing was my initial and important task of finding and defeating the Evil being. and so every time I was teleported to a dimension, at first I get awe-struck by the type of existence I'm in and how the world is, and then i remember a glimpse of what i am here for and focus all my brain power to reconstruct my intentions and thoughts to fit into the current form i am in. sometime I succeed and other I fail ( thankfully i had friends to throw me to another dimension so i don’t lose myself in whatever form I’m in).
Maybe it is not clear what I’m trying to convey, but i want you to think how hard is to align the goals of an algae, a multi-dimensional brain, a Super AI and a human. I also find it difficult to write about. However, the experience solidified a concept that I can only describe as Isomorphic intentions. so in that trippy stat, i was creating an isomorphic map between the minds of creatures i was so I can map my intention from one form to another. So my goal or intention remained the same up to an isomorphism, not matter the state i was.

In mathematics, an isomorphism is a structure-preserving mapping between two structures of the same type that can be reversed by an inverse mapping. If such mapping exist between two structures, they are called isomorphic and isomorphic structures cannot be distinguished using structures only. another way to describe two isomorphic objects, is to say they are the same up to an isomorphism.

sometimes i encounter a concept in one area or field that is strikingly similar to another in an unrelated field. it is a strange realization that pushes me toward introspection of the underlying truth of our reality. how is it that these seemingly unrelated concepts map so perfectly to another even when they are from different universes? 

## Path to Mastery is Isomorhpic

I was listening to an audio book of Bruce lee biography, learning about his life and how he revolutionized martial arts by combining different kinds of styles and integrating them to his own unique style. What stuck with me was a specific segment of the audiobook, it was about how a beginner must learn different kinds of punches, upper cut, side punch, direct punch.. etc. how he must practice them separately and relentlessly. How during a spar or a practice he must chose carefully what punch to use and when, and how they are different. and then during his training years, all these different punches are engraved in him, and the lines between them blurs. after that he reaches a point where he simply punches, there is no difference nor nuance in them, there is only the punch.

I was reading The Score by C. Thi, it was a book about how scores alter our value structure whether for good or for bad. And it would benefits us all to be aware of this value creep. Still not done with book, I listened to his podcast appearance during my run. He was reflecting about his rock-climbing hobby, about how he started rock climbing as a way to exercise, and during the first 4 years it was exactly what he hoped for. It was a hard and exhausting, and no doubt improved his health overall. After his fourth year, he noticed that he gained a different appreciation and love for the game, he enjoyed the fluid movements his body was making, the smooth flow of jumps and twists and getting in-tune with his body. Which made him love his body more.
listening to that, my mind immediately jumped back to Bruce lee's punch. it seems to me that Mr.Thi appreciation and love could only come after the agonizing four years of practice and training. It was not a shift in mindset nor a change of heart but after enough practice he gained mastery of the basics, thus unlocking a different game.

I have listens/read several times about how experts develop some kind of intuition and gut reaction that is more often correct than not (which is not true for non-experts). the theory is that this intuition is built by years and years of training and practice, eventually it is all integrated in their brain and is done unconsciously. 

what I'm reaching at is that the path of master is the same up to an isomorphism. In order to master anything, one must suffer the humiliation and struggles of being a complete beginner, and only after enough time training and practicing another level can be achieved. "To transcend retardation you must first embrace it" -incorrectly attributed to Miyamoto Musashi. 

## My Resolution going forward

I recently read an amazing [article](https://substack.com/home/post/p-183753276) on Substack about How AI is changing how people will engage with math. It delves into why it is useful to learn high-level hard math, and how it will always be a human endeavour. The article solidified certain ideas I had about transferable knowledge but couldn't express. Learning difficult concepts help creates new neurological pathways allowing different models of thinking, which is then utilized to solve novel problems no matter the discipline.

Reading the article made me think back to my mushroom trip, which made me experience how concepts and ideas can be transferrable between very different planes of existence (They are isomorphic). I am forever grateful for the experience, because knowing something and experiencing it is very different. knowing that a cup of coffee is hot, and touching it hence experiencing that it is hot are very different. having said that, I have decided to continuously learn new math concepts and theories. It doesn't have to be grandeur, but it should be hard enough to test my limits of thinking. 

This will benefit me directly by keeping my mind fresh and sharp. and since I believe reality is mathematical in essence or at least has a well-defined structure, these complex mathematical concepts may be isomorphic to real-life complexities. Thus, if one absorbs the knowledge truly, one may find an isomorphic map to an existing problem he is facing and use the newly developed skills and techniques to address them.
