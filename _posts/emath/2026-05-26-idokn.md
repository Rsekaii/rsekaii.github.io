### "Real knowledge is to know the extent of one's ignorance." — Confucius 
A common wisdom everyone knows, but it is not appreciated enough as evident by the rampant mistrust of expertise nowadays. Even among experts in their fields, it is common to misjudge their level of ignorance of another field. I mean how could he? understanding what he is ignorant about would take a significant amount of investment in another field that is rarely benefit him directly.

### "We live on an island surrounded by a sea of ignorance. As our island of knowledge grows, so does the shore of our ignorance." - John Archibald Wheeler

I was STEM-brained through and through, up until I studied for my CFA exam. I unlocked an angle to view the world that I was oblivious to, and what an angle it is. it wouldn't be a complete stretch to claim all human activities are downstream of The Economy, and Finance is its main instrument. Upon understanding the fundamentals, a new depth unlocked in my analysis.

It’s hard to remember what I thought of these concepts, since the model by which I tried to understand them is now overwritten with a much more appropriate and doctrinal model. Like how a doctor perceive a disease before and after medical school.  However, because economy touches everyone’s life it is hard to claim ignorance on the topic and thus, it is a conversation in every majlis.

What does it mean that a country loses global trust? it sounds vague and interpretable. but for a 30-year country bond's yield to increase significantly? it is a different type of insight. Why does it always feel like money is worth less than it did when we were young? obviously, it was because we had less "بركة"! and to my surprise, it is a feature not a bug. Banks suddenly had valid reason to require interest on loans, instead of being evil corporate bastards! Market efficiency, Supply and demand, Fiscal Policy are all terms I thought I understood. Government debt was self-explanatory; the government bought a new car probably. 

### “I don’t know what I don’t know”

I was sponsored by ADNOC during my undergraduate degree, yet they didn't hire me despite my exceptional GPA. it should’ve a smooth-hire as evident by all the other cohorts (before and after me) I was frustrated, especially since Military service was encroaching. Why is God punishing _me_? 

what I was not aware is that OPEC's increased oil production to fight for it's market share, after USA-Shell made a technological breakthrough increasing it's supply significantly. This over-supply, coupled with decreased demand in China (major market) resulted in an unprecedented drop in oil prices. This impacted the economy of oil-dependant countries. The impact was so severe on oil producing companies (like ADNOC) that they had to restructure their business model, and I was part of the cost they cut that year. And here I thought it was because I masturbated a lot. 

### كُلّما أدّبني الدهرُ أراني نَقْصَ عقلي..  فإذا ما ازدَدْتُ عِلمًا، زادَني عِلمًا بجهلي. — الإمام الشافعي

The science behind machine learning and AI is magnificent and fascinating, and yet it is misunderstood. In my experience it is extremely hard to point and highlight the difference between what the current version of the big LLMS is doing, and what the public think they are doing. Tech leaders are incentivised to blur the lines and morph definitions. A next token prediction model is not doing any thinking no matter how complex the model is or how surprising the outcome is. To most people the line I’m drawing is insignificant, a distinction without difference. However, only by understanding the difference will one decide to keep his mind clean and not outsource his thinking. Only then would he be able to utilize these tools efficiently and properly. 

While writing this, I remember a series of [lectures](https://youtu.be/NNnIGh9g6fA?si=vxVGxSCD1xPAjPPp) by prof Robert Sapolsky from Stanford. In which explained human marriage using different lenses; hormones, neuron, sociology, psychology, political and evolutionary. He bet his students that by the end of his lectures no one will believe in free-will. It was fascinating to me how different profession viewed the same problem differently, and how all scientists tend to reduce the world to their specific field. It was a testimony to how different world-views can all be valid and useful depending on the context, some more useful than others. 

Reflecting on my own experience, I should be humble going forward when talking about things outside my ‘island of knowledge’. I will aim to follow experts whenever I can. But I am also excited about learning new areas of knowledge, earn those glasses through which I see the world. How will my understanding change if I learn psychology or sociology? History perhaps? Politics? It is all very exciting. 

### “I am the wisest man alive, for I know one thing, and that is that I know nothing.” - Socrates
